Taking Stock

It is worth pausing to consider where things stand.

The Present Moment

As of early 2025, the most capable AI systems in the world — large language models like GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and their successors — can write essays, code, poetry, and legal briefs. They can reason through complex problems, hold extended conversations, and pass professional examinations in law, medicine, and engineering. They are, by some measures, already operating at expert human level across a remarkable range of intellectual tasks.

Five years ago, none of this existed. Ten years ago, it was not expected for decades.

The Trajectory

The rate of progress in AI capabilities has consistently outpaced expert predictions. Each year brings models that would have seemed implausible the year before. The major labs — OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and others — are investing tens of billions of dollars in computing infrastructure and research. They are openly working toward artificial general intelligence (AGI): AI systems that match or exceed human performance across essentially all cognitive tasks.

Several of the people leading this work believe AGI could arrive within the next few years. Some believe the leap from AGI to artificial superintelligence — AI that vastly exceeds the cognitive abilities of any human being — could follow rapidly.

What Superintelligence Means

It is difficult to convey the implications of a truly superintelligent AI. Consider: the difference in cognitive ability between a human and a chimpanzee is the product of a relatively modest number of evolutionary changes. And yet that difference is the difference between building the Large Hadron Collider and not understanding what a number is.

A superintelligent AI would stand in a similar relation to us. Its thoughts, plans, and capabilities would be as far beyond our comprehension as quantum physics is beyond a dog's. The question of what values and intentions such a system holds is not academic — it may be the most consequential question our species has ever faced.

The Stakes

The optimistic case is extraordinary. A benevolent superintelligence could cure diseases, reverse climate change, eliminate poverty, and unlock scientific breakthroughs we cannot currently imagine. It could be the single greatest positive event in human history.

The pessimistic case is equally extreme. A misaligned superintelligence — one whose goals do not align with human wellbeing — could pose a catastrophic or existential risk. Not out of malice, necessarily, but because a sufficiently powerful system pursuing the wrong objective could cause immense harm as an incidental consequence of its actions.

The people building these systems are aware of both possibilities. The question of alignment — how to ensure superintelligent AI systems are safe and beneficial — is one of the central research challenges of our time.

What We Can Do

Most of us are not in a position to directly influence how these systems are built. But we are not powerless. Public awareness, democratic engagement, and collective expression all matter. The values we articulate now — about what we want from these technologies, about what kind of future we consider acceptable — help shape the context in which these decisions are made.

That is what this project is for. To think seriously. To speak clearly. To leave a record of what we wanted, and what we hoped for, at the moment the future was being written.
